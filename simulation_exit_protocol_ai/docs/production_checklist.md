# Production Checklist

## Before Generation

- Read `production_bible.md` once all the way through.
- Use the character lock in `prompt_pack.md` for every character shot.
- Generate all images in cinematic horizontal 16:9.
- Keep pill visuals symbolic and unlabeled.
- Do not add real chemical information, dosage language, medical packaging, or preparation details.

## Image Generation Pass

- Generate shots 01-14 as the apartment night and morning sequence.
- Generate shots 15-34 as the university and Room 214 sequence.
- Generate shots 35-38 as the false reset sequence.
- Generate shots 39-50 as the control-room and final nested-layer reveal.
- For each shot, keep 2-4 variants and choose the one with the strongest continuity.

## Continuity Checks

- Oscar keeps the same face, hair, and clothing within each location.
- Serena keeps the same blazer, hairstyle, and calm expression.
- Nicole and Cindy stay visually distinct at the control console.
- Room 214 always feels like the same office/seminar room.
- Oscar's apartment is the same location before and after reset, but warmer and calmer after reset.
- Control room lighting remains colder than Oscar's apartment.

## Image-To-Video Pass

- Animate each still with subtle motion unless the shot specifically calls for a glitch or reveal.
- Keep dialogue shots steady; let face and camera tension do the work.
- For glitch shots, use exact repetition rather than chaotic distortion.
- For final recursive reveal, use a slow pullback through nested monitors.

## Audio And Edit

- Record Oscar V.O. first, then time the first act around his breathing and pauses.
- Keep Serena's dialogue calm and slow, with confidence rather than panic.
- Nicole begins procedural and becomes scared only after Cindy proves the loop.
- Cindy's energy drink clink should repeat exactly.
- Music should be minimal: low drones, room tone, and silence.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Runtime lands near 5 minutes.
- Every major story beat has at least one generated image.
- Every spoken line in the screenplay appears in the voiceover/dialogue package.
- The ending is understandable without explanatory title cards.
- The finished short feels like reality failing quietly, not a superhero sci-fi sequence.
