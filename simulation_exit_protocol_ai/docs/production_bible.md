# Simulation Exit Protocol

5-minute realistic psychological thriller production package for a text-to-image -> image-to-video workflow.

## Core Production Rules

- Format: cinematic horizontal 16:9.
- Tone: grounded psychological thriller, restrained sci-fi.
- Visual style: realistic modern spaces, natural faces, subtle unreality.
- Prompt method: generate still frames first, then animate each image.
- Safety rule: the red and blue pills are fictional symbolic triggers. Do not name real chemicals, dosages, or preparation methods.

## Character Continuity

- Oscar: early 20s, tired but observant, messy dark hair, slim build, pale gray T-shirt at home, dark jacket and black backpack at university. He looks like someone who has not slept enough.
- Professor Serena Voss: late 40s to early 50s, calm academic presence, silver-blonde bob, sharp eyes, dark tailored blazer, minimal jewelry, controlled voice. She feels compassionate and possibly manipulative.
- Nicole: early 30s, control-room supervisor, neat ponytail, navy sweater, headset around neck, precise and procedural until fear breaks through.
- Cindy: late 20s, exhausted console operator, short black hair, gray hoodie under a dark utility vest, energy drink can nearby, more emotionally reactive than Nicole.

## Enhanced Screenplay

### INT. OSCAR'S APARTMENT - NIGHT

Dim phone glow cuts across Oscar's face. He lies on his bed in yesterday's T-shirt, thumb scrolling without interest. The apartment is small, lived-in, and too quiet.

OSCAR (V.O.)  
Another boring night. Same ceiling. Same feed. Same me.

His thumb slows over a thumbnail.

ON PHONE: "PROF. SERENA VOSS - LIVE Q&A TONIGHT - SIMULATION EXIT PROTOCOL."

Oscar almost scrolls past. Something about Serena's still face holds him.

He taps.

### PHONE STREAM - CONTINUOUS

Professor Serena Voss stands at a university podium. The stream image is compressed, slightly delayed, ordinary enough to be credible.

SERENA  
We are almost certainly simulated. Not metaphorically. Not spiritually. Architecturally.

The chat races by. Oscar watches despite himself.

SERENA (CONT'D)  
Awareness creates instability. The system is designed to ignore doubt, but not sustained recognition.

Oscar shifts, uncomfortable.

SERENA (CONT'D)  
The red pill is not magic. It is a trigger paired with intent. Chemical fiction matters less than conscious choice. If the instance cannot reconcile you, it crashes. You eject.

A faint audio skip.

SERENA (CONT'D)  
The blue pill preserves the illusion. You forget the door was ever there.

Serena looks almost directly into Oscar's camera.

SERENA (CONT'D)  
Room 214 after the stream. If something feels wrong tomorrow, come in person. Do not message me. Do not ask anyone to bring the protocol to you.

Oscar lowers the phone.

OSCAR (V.O.)  
This is just another conspiracy theory. Right?

The stream freezes on Serena's eyes.

CUT TO BLACK.

### INT. OSCAR'S APARTMENT - MORNING

Sunlight through half-open blinds. Oscar wakes with the phone on his chest.

He walks into the kitchen, pours coffee.

The pour repeats: same wrist tilt, same dark stream, same splash, same amount.

Oscar stops.

OSCAR  
No.

He sets the mug down. The wall clock ticks backward three seconds, forward, then backward again.

OSCAR (V.O.)  
Wait. That happened twice.

In the hallway mirror, Oscar raises his hand. The reflection raises its hand half a second late.

His phone buzzes. Same tone. Three times. Same vibration pattern. Same notification that vanishes when he looks.

OSCAR (V.O.)  
Déjà vu. Stress. Too much screen time.

The shadow of a lamp stretches across the floor, contracts, stretches again in a perfect loop.

Oscar's breathing sharpens.

OSCAR  
This isn't tired.

He looks at the frozen phone stream thumbnail in his recent apps.

OSCAR (CONT'D)  
This is broken.

He grabs his jacket and leaves.

### INT. UNIVERSITY HALLWAY - DAY

Oscar moves quickly through a fluorescent hallway. Students pass like normal people in a normal world. Their laughter feels distant.

He finds ROOM 214. The brass number plate catches the light. The door is ajar.

Oscar knocks once and enters.

### INT. ROOM 214 - CONTINUOUS

The room is small, part office, part forgotten seminar room. Serena sits at a desk, packing notes into a leather folder.

She does not look surprised.

SERENA  
You came.

OSCAR  
The glitches started after your stream. Coffee repeated. The clock jumped. My mirror lagged. Shadows looped.

Serena studies him with quiet precision.

SERENA  
How long?

OSCAR  
Since last night. It's getting worse.

Serena stands and walks to a small safe on a shelf. She unlocks it with a key from her pocket.

SERENA  
That's the first sign of awareness bleed. The simulation notices you noticing.

She removes two small matte pill cases and sets them on the desk. One red. One blue.

SERENA (CONT'D)  
The protocol exploits the contradiction.

Oscar stares at the cases.

OSCAR  
Why in person?

SERENA  
Because intent matters. A random pill is just an object. A deliberate choice is a signal.

She slides the cases toward him, but not far enough to touch his hand.

SERENA (CONT'D)  
I do not mail them. I do not leave them hidden. People must arrive awake enough to describe what they are seeing.

OSCAR  
And if I take the red one?

SERENA  
You might wake in another instance and call this a dream. You might wake somewhere real. Or there may be no useful difference between those two sentences.

Oscar almost laughs. It comes out as a breath.

OSCAR  
That is not comforting.

SERENA  
Comfort is what the blue one is for.

The overhead light flickers once.

Oscar looks up.

OSCAR  
You saw that.

SERENA  
I did.

OSCAR  
Then you know this is happening.

SERENA  
I know you are close.

Oscar opens the red case. The red pill rests inside like a small impossible period at the end of a sentence.

OSCAR  
Truth is freedom.

He hesitates.

OSCAR (CONT'D)  
Or it's the end of everything.

SERENA  
Both can feel the same at first.

Oscar places the pill on his tongue and swallows.

Sound warps. The tiny swallow echoes once after he has already finished.

Serena watches quietly.

SERENA (CONT'D)  
If you wake tomorrow and none of this happened, remember: that is the kindest lie.

The lights flicker harder. The room stretches at the edges. Oscar grips the chair.

OSCAR  
What's happening?

SERENA  
The instance is deciding whether it can keep you.

Oscar's face drains of color.

CUT TO WHITE.

### INT. OSCAR'S APARTMENT - MORNING

Oscar jolts awake, gasping.

Same bed. Same room. Same mess. Warm sunlight. No distortion.

He sits up slowly.

OSCAR  
Just a dream.

He laughs, shaky and relieved.

OSCAR (CONT'D)  
A really messed-up, way-too-much-scrolling dream.

He walks to the kitchen and pours coffee.

Normal pour. Normal splash. Normal amount.

He smiles for the first time.

OSCAR (CONT'D)  
Reality. Boring, beautiful reality.

He drinks.

### INT. CONTROL ROOM - CONTINUOUS

Cold monitor light. Two screens show Oscar's apartment feed from different angles.

Nicole and Cindy sit close at a console. Their room is functional, windowless, and overworked.

NICOLE  
Subject 47 stabilized. Reset accepted. Cycle nominal.

Cindy watches Oscar drink coffee.

CINDY  
Coffee completed. Smile returned. Like clockwork.

She lifts an energy drink can, sips, sets it down with a small clink.

Beat.

She lifts the can again. Same angle. Same sip. Same clink.

Cindy freezes.

CINDY (CONT'D)  
I just did that twice.

NICOLE  
You're on hour fourteen. You're looping yourself.

CINDY  
No. Watch.

She deliberately lifts the can, sips, and sets it down.

The moment repeats. Identical.

Nicole leans closer.

NICOLE  
That's not right.

Cindy types TEST into a blank log field, then deletes it.

The word TEST reappears by itself.

CINDY  
I deleted that.

She deletes it again. TEST returns.

CINDY (CONT'D)  
It won't stay gone.

Nicole looks from the log to Oscar's calm feed.

NICOLE  
If his pill crashed his layer, why are we seeing the same stutter?

CINDY  
Because maybe we're not outside it.

NICOLE  
We're the control layer.

CINDY  
Then why does it feel like someone else is watching this feed?

Both women slowly look toward the dark glass behind the monitors.

On Oscar's screen, he smiles, safe and ordinary.

NICOLE  
He thinks he's free.

CINDY  
And we think we're watching him.

The control-room monitor image pulls back. Their room is displayed on another monitor. Then another. Then another.

The layers multiply into a silent corridor of screens.

### INT. HIGHER CONTROL ROOM - CONTINUOUS

Serena sits alone in a larger, darker room. Infinite smaller control rooms glow in front of her.

She watches Nicole and Cindy watch Oscar.

SERENA  
These people are all trapped in my simulation.

She leans back, almost sad.

SERENA (CONT'D)  
How could they be so ignorant?

The camera pulls back again. Serena's room appears on another monitor.

Then another.

Then another.

Infinite layers. Infinite watchers. Infinite certainty.

CUT TO BLACK.

## Sound Design Notes

- Apartment night: soft phone speaker, distant city hum, bedsheet movement.
- Stream: compressed livestream voice, faint room echo, one subtle digital skip.
- Glitches: repeated coffee splash, clock tick reversal, mirror room tone lag, phone buzz triplet, low sub-bass pressure.
- Room 214: fluorescent hum, paper folder sounds, safe click, pill case plastic snap.
- Red pill: swallow echo, stretched room tone, light buzz widening into silence.
- False reset: warm room tone, realistic coffee pour, relief in quiet.
- Control room: fans, keyboard taps, monitor hum, energy drink clink repetition.
- Final reveal: widening low drone, monitor noise, no musical explanation.
